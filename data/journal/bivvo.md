Every event has a version of the same story. An organizer is managing registrations, volunteers are waiting for instructions, and attendees are trying to figure out where they need to be. Important information is scattered across messages, spreadsheets, and different platforms.

Nothing is necessarily broken. But everyone is working with a different piece of the picture.

As the event gets closer, small communication gaps turn into bigger coordination problems. A volunteer misses an update. An organizer has to manually track applications. An attendee doesn't know what happens next.

The problem isn't always the event itself. It's everything happening behind it.

## Coordination is the real challenge

Organizing an event involves more than choosing a venue and publishing a schedule. There are people to onboard, roles to assign, applications to manage, and information that needs to reach the right person at the right time.

For organizers, keeping track of everything can become a manual process. Volunteers need a clear way to discover opportunities and apply. Attendees need a straightforward experience without having to chase information.

When these experiences are disconnected, even a well-planned event can become difficult to manage.

That's the gap Bivvo was built to address.

## Three things every event platform should get right

- **One connected experience:** Organizers, volunteers, and attendees should have a dedicated experience that fits their role instead of navigating unrelated tools.
- **A structured event workflow:** Creating, managing, drafting, and publishing events should happen through a clear and organized process.
- **Simpler onboarding and profiles:** People should be able to sign up, create their profiles, and access the features relevant to their role without unnecessary friction.

These aren't just convenience features. They're the foundation of a platform that makes event coordination easier to manage as things grow.

## What a connected experience looks like

Bivvo brings event organizers, volunteers, and attendees together through a role-based platform.

Organizers can create events, manage drafts, and publish event information. Volunteers and attendees have their own onboarding experiences and profiles, designed around how they interact with events.

The platform also supports email and Google sign-in, profile management, and image uploads, creating a more structured experience from the moment someone joins.

Instead of treating every participant the same, Bivvo is built around the different roles people play in an event.

The goal isn't to add another layer of software to event management. It's to make the existing process feel less scattered.

That's the whole idea behind Bivvo. Not more coordination overhead. Less of it.
